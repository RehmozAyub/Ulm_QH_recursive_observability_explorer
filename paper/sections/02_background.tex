\section{Background}
\label{sec:background}

\subsection{The Drake equation and what exoplanet science settled}

The Drake equation organises the expected number of communicative civilizations
as a product,
\begin{equation}
  N = R_* f_p n_e f_l f_i f_c L ,
  \label{eq:drake}
\end{equation}
and it is best read as a map of our ignorance rather than as a calculator
\citep{drake1992}. Exoplanet surveys have since pinned down its leading
astrophysical factors rather well: planets are common, and potentially habitable
planets are no longer speculative
\citep{borucki2016,batalha2014,dressing2015,kopparapu2013,seager2013}.
\Established

So the uncertainty has migrated rightward. It now sits in the emergence of life
and intelligence, and above all in $L$, the interval over which a civilization is
detectable. \citet{prantzos2020} shows explicitly that $L$ dominates the whole
inference. That is the term this framework acts on, by refusing to let it be a
scalar.

\subsection{Great Filter and existential risk}

The Great Filter frames the problem as a bottleneck: somewhere between
non-living matter and a long-lived expansive civilization there must be a step
that is extraordinarily improbable \citep{hanson1998filter}. Whether that step is
behind us or ahead of us carries obvious stakes, and the existential-risk
literature explores the second possibility at length
\citep{bostrom2002,bostrom2013,ord2020,haggstrom2016}. \citet{cirkovic2018}
surveys the philosophical terrain and \citet{verendel2017} give a Bayesian
treatment.

But the Great Filter is a statement about where probability mass goes, not about
dynamics. It says nothing about how a civilization moves through the bottleneck,
and nothing at all about what it looks like from outside while it is doing so.
That is the gap a dynamical model can fill.

\subsection{Technosignatures and the search}

What could actually be detected has been studied since \citet{cocconi1959} and
\citet{dyson1960}. Energy-use signatures scale with the Kardashev classification
\citep{kardashev1964}; infrared searches for large-scale energy capture have
returned null results at survey scale
\citep{wright2014ghat,griffith2015,garrett2015}; and the field has increasingly
formalised how to reason about coverage of the search space
\citep{tarter2001,wright2018state,sheikh2020,wright2022haystack}.

Two points matter for what follows. Different signature classes correspond to
different civilizational behaviours --- waste heat, narrowband beacons,
propulsion, megastructures, atmospheric markers --- so ``detectability'' is not
one quantity, and treating it as one throws away most of the information. And
the searches conducted so far cover a genuinely small fraction of the available
parameter space, which is exactly why $\Psearch$ has to appear explicitly in any
honest inference \citep{wright2022haystack}. \Established

\subsection{Expansion, percolation and timing}

If interstellar settlement is feasible at all, the galaxy should be crossed
quickly on astronomical timescales \citep{hart1975,tipler1980,armstrong2013},
which sharpens the paradox considerably. Percolation and agent-based treatments
soften it again by letting settlement stall \citep{landis1998,forgan2009}.
Anthropic and hard-steps arguments address why we find ourselves so early
\citep{carter1983,watson2008,kipping2020}.

Then there is the grabby-aliens analysis of \citet{hanson2021grabby}, which
makes a sharp observation: if loud expanding civilizations are what explain
human earliness, then quiet ones have to be rare as well. That is the most
direct challenge to any low-observability account of the Great Silence, ours
very much included, and we return to it in \S\ref{sec:discussion}.

\subsection{Recursive intelligence}

The possibility that capability improves the machinery of capability has been
discussed since \citet{good1965}, and elaborated with attention to control and
alignment by \citet{bostrom2014}, \citet{russell2019}, \citet{omohundro2008} and
\citet{yudkowsky2008}. Quantum computation is relevant to this framework through
that channel and no other --- as one possible accelerant of the recursive
improvement loop \citep{shor1994,preskill2018,nielsen2010} --- and we are
explicit that both its magnitude and its sign of effect are unconstrained.

\subsection{The gap}

Across all of these literatures, detectability is treated as a static property:
a coefficient, a lifetime, or a survey limit. Where it is allowed to change, the
change is imposed as a chosen profile. What is missing is a formulation in which
observability has its own equation of motion, is coupled to the same processes
that drive capability and regulation, and is constrained to stay physical.
Supplying that, and then testing it seriously, is what this paper is for.
