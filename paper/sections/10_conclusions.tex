\section{Conclusions}
\label{sec:conclusions}

The Fermi paradox is usually modelled by varying how many civilizations arise.
We have argued it should also be modelled by varying how long they stay
externally observable, and that observability belongs in the state vector with
its own equation of motion rather than in a coefficient.

Three results follow.

First, once existence, signal production and search coverage are pulled apart in
Eq.~\eqref{eq:decomp}, non-observation constrains their product and not $\Ntrue$
alone. That is a logical point rather than a discovery, but it is elided
routinely, and it determines what a model of the paradox should vary in the
first place. \Established

Second, an observability equation has to be built so that it cannot leave the
physical range. Writing suppression as a fractional rate acting on the emitted
signal, above an explicit thermodynamic floor, achieves that by construction
(Proposition~\ref{prop:obs}), and treating erosion the same way confines
regulatory capacity to the unit interval (Proposition~\ref{prop:reg}). These are
not cosmetic repairs. The uncorrected formulation produced --- and confidently
labelled ``optimized low-observable'' --- a quiet civilization whose
observability was $-1431$.

Third, and this is the modelling result we would defend hardest,
peak-then-decline observability does not have to be assumed. Because the
quasi-steady observability is a ratio of production to suppression, the decline
appears on its own whenever suppression scales faster in capability than
production (Eq.~\eqref{eq:decline}). A low-observability account of the Great
Silence built this way does not assume its own conclusion, and it can be
attacked where an argument should be attacked: on whether the scaling separation
is plausible, rather than on the choice of a convenient curve. \Plausible

We have also reported a failure. The Lambert $W$ threshold that named and
motivated this work is not a property of the integrated system at all. The
natural balance cancels $\Icap$ and leaves a logarithm, and recovering Lambert
structure needs an auxiliary balance we have to declare rather than derive
(\S\ref{sec:lambert}). We keep the boundary as a diagnostic and depend on it
nowhere.

Finally, this framework is verified rather than merely implemented. Closed-form
solutions, an independent high-order integrator, quadrature of the observability
equation, and property-based testing over 300 randomised parameter sets together
establish that the equations described here are the equations being solved. They
establish nothing about whether those equations describe a real civilization,
and we have worked to keep verification and validation from being confused.

The framework does not solve the Fermi paradox and was never meant to. Its
purpose is to make a class of arguments about cosmic silence precise enough to
be \emph{wrong}: to say which assumptions produce which regimes, which
conclusions survive a change of functional form, and what observation would
count against them. The most useful next steps are a population-level treatment
that can be set against grabby-aliens constraints, a collapse term that produces
trajectories instead of flags, and a nondimensionalisation that cuts the
parameter count down to the few groups that actually govern the behaviour.
