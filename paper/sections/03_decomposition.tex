\section{Separating existence, detectability and observation}
\label{sec:decomp}

\subsection{The decomposition}

Let $\Ntrue$ be the number of technological civilizations that exist in the
volume and epoch we care about. Write $\Psurv$ for the chance that such a
civilization is still around in the phase we could detect at all, $h(\Obs)$ for
the chance it is putting out a signature strong enough to register given its
external observability $\Obs$, and $\Psearch$ for the chance our searches have
actually looked where that signature is. Then
\begin{equation}
  \boxed{\;\Nobs \;=\; \Ntrue \,\cdot\, \Psurv \,\cdot\, h(\Obs) \,\cdot\, \Psearch\;}
  \label{eq:decomp}
\end{equation}
with $h$ a saturating map from signature strength to detection probability. We
take
\begin{equation}
  h(\Obs) \;=\; 1 - e^{-\kappa \Obs},
  \label{eq:hdet}
\end{equation}
where $\kappa$ sets instrument sensitivity, and we write
$\Pdet = h(\Obs)\,\Psearch$ for the detection probability of a single
civilization.

There is no theory in Eq.~\eqref{eq:decomp}. It is bookkeeping. Its whole value
is that it refuses to let four different mechanisms hide inside one number.
\Established{} Figure~\ref{fig:venn} shows how the sets nest, and
Figure~\ref{fig:drakemap} shows where this framework acts on the Drake
equation.

\subsection{Why this matters for inference}

Suppose a survey reports nothing. Under Eq.~\eqref{eq:decomp} the likelihood of
that outcome depends on $\Psearch$ and on how $\Obs$ is spread across the
population --- not on $\Ntrue$ by itself. Two very different universes give the
same null result: one where civilizations are rare, and one where they are
everywhere but mostly quiet during the sliver of time we can sample. Telling
them apart needs a model of how $\Obs$ is distributed and how it changes, which
is what \S\ref{sec:model} supplies. \Established

\subsection{What observability is, and is not}

By $\Obs$ we mean \emph{external observability}: the strength of whatever leaves
the system, adding up waste heat, leakage, deliberate emission, expansion
footprint and engineered structures. It is not energy use and it is not
capability. A civilization can be enormously capable and rather quiet; it can
also be modest and extremely loud.

And $\Obs$ can get small, but it cannot get to zero. Using energy has
thermodynamic consequences, and claiming otherwise would need physics we have no
right to assume \citep{dyson1960,kardashev1964}. So we build that in: $\Obs$ is
bounded below by a strictly positive floor $\Ofloor$
(Proposition~\ref{prop:obs}). In this framework low observability always means
hard to find. It never means gone. \Established
