\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}

\subsection{Relation to existing solution classes}

This framework is not a rival to the standard families of Fermi-paradox
explanations. It is a way of writing several of them in a common state space,
where they can be compared instead of merely asserted at one another.

\paragraph{Great Filter.} A filter is a statement about where probability mass is
lost. Here, a filter ahead of us shows up as a region of parameter space from
which trajectories reach the collapse proxy. The model also makes something
explicit that is usually left implicit: a filter acting on \emph{observability}
rather than on survival is equally possible, and using non-detection alone you
could never tell the two apart \citep{hanson1998filter,cirkovic2018}.
\Hypothetical

\paragraph{Grabby aliens.} \citet{hanson2021grabby} argue that if loud, expanding
civilizations explain human earliness, quiet civilizations have to be rare too.
This is the sharpest challenge to any low-observability account, ours included,
and we do not claim to answer it. What the framework contributes is somewhere to
state the disagreement precisely: the grabby-aliens argument constrains the
\emph{joint} distribution of expansion behaviour and timing, which in our terms
is a constraint on the population distribution of the expansion term $X$ and on
the branch probabilities at stage~3 --- and both of those are quantities this
model takes as given rather than derives. Coupling the two is the obvious next
piece of work.

\paragraph{Zoo and non-interference hypotheses.} These correspond to a stealth
term $S$ that is large and deliberate \citep{ball1973zoo,bracewell1960}. The
framework accommodates them comfortably, but it cannot tell deliberate quietness
from emergent efficiency, because both enter $\Sigma$ in exactly the same way.
That degeneracy is worth stating rather than glossing: behavioural and
thermodynamic explanations of quietness are not separable by observability
alone, and no amount of better instrumentation changes that.

\paragraph{Percolation and expansion limits.} Models in which settlement stalls
\citep{landis1998,forgan2009,armstrong2013} act on $X$, the expansion
contribution to production, and are compatible with the ``inward'' variant in
Table~\ref{tab:registry}.

\subsection{Thermodynamic discipline}

The floor $\Ofloor$ is not a numerical convenience. Any civilization that uses
energy radiates, and true invisibility would need assumptions about physics we
have no basis for making \citep{dyson1960,kardashev1964}.

Putting the floor into the equation rather than into the prose buys something
concrete: it makes the strong claim unavailable. You cannot accidentally
conclude from this model that a civilization vanished. You can only conclude that
it became hard to detect at a stated sensitivity $\kappa$ and a stated coverage
$\Psearch$. \Established

That is exactly the difference between the corrected and uncorrected
formulations of \S\ref{sec:derived}. The uncorrected version permitted
arbitrarily negative observability, and so permitted --- and in fact produced,
and cheerfully labelled --- a conclusion its own thermodynamic commitments
forbid.

\subsection{Implications for search strategy}

If the low-observability regime is populated at all, it carries a testable
consequence. In that regime, production is dominated by residual energy use
rather than by broadcast or expansion, because the directed and efficient
channels are precisely the ones suppression acts on hardest. Search programmes
sensitive to waste heat and infrared excess would then keep their sensitivity
where narrowband beacon searches lose theirs
\citep{dyson1960,wright2014ghat,garrett2015}.

This is a conditional, not a recommendation. It holds if the regime is
populated, and that claim is labelled \Hypothetical. Its value is that it is the
kind of statement that could turn out to be wrong: sustained null results from
infrared surveys at improving sensitivity would push against it
\citep{griffith2015,wright2022haystack}.

\subsection{On the role of recursive and quantum capability}

The model treats $\Arec$ as an exogenous driver and does not pretend to explain
where recursive capability comes from. Quantum computation enters only as one
possible contributor to that driver --- a way the simulation, search and
optimisation problems gating technological progress might be accelerated
\citep{shor1994,preskill2018}.

We attach no magnitude to this, and there is a more interesting point to make
than a caveat: its sign of effect on \emph{observability} is not determined at
all. Faster capability growth raises production through $E$ and $\Icap$, but it
also raises suppression through $C$ and $S$, and Eq.~\eqref{eq:Ostar} says only
the ratio matters. A civilization that computes far more efficiently might end
up louder or quieter, depending entirely on which term scales faster. Anyone
asserting a direction here is asserting a scaling law they have not written
down. \Speculative
